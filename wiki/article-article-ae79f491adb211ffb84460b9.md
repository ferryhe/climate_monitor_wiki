## Registry article-version summary

A new WEF article (April 2026) examines how systemic risks — including climate change, cyber threats, and geopolitical instability — act as a hidden tax on economic growth, and how the insurance industry's expanding role can help build societal resilience before shocks occur. The article highlights advanced risk modelling, data analytics, and early-warning systems as tools insurers can deploy. For climate actuaries, the piece underscores the growing expectation that insurers will serve as shock absorbers and long-term resilience partners for governments and corporates alike.

## Report observation: climate-monitor-2026-04-18.md

Systemic risk arising from climate volatility, geopolitical fragmentation, technological disruption and public-health crises behaves like a hidden tax by raising investment costs, discouraging innovation and transmitting shocks through interconnected systems. Insurance can convert uncertainty into resilience through risk quantification, pooling and transfer, advanced modelling and early warning, adaptation investment and public-private risk sharing.

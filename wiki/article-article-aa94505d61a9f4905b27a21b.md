WMO describes how an upstream ice-and-rock avalanche and possible temporary river blockage may have contributed to Nepal's flash floods; the trigger was still under investigation. Rapid downstream water-level rises and damaged monitoring stations illustrate cascading, cross-border hazards and the need for stronger observations, data exchange and actionable early warnings.

Flood tragedy in Nepal highlights cross-border and cascading risks
IN WINDOW 27 AUG 2026 PRESS RELEASE
An ice-and-rock avalanche on the Tibet border triggered a flash flood on 26 August; 359 deaths were confirmed
by 27 August with around 10,000 households affected, and the Trishuli River rose nine metres in thirty minutes.
A textbook cascading, cross-border cryosphere hazard that sits poorly in conventional flood catastrophe models,
and material for Himalayan hydropower and infrastructure exposures.

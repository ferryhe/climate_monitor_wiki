## Registry article-version summary

A detailed analysis from Columbia Climate School documents how Africa receives less than $14 billion per year in adaptation finance against an estimated need exceeding $100 billion, with more than half of available flows arriving as interest-bearing loans rather than grants. The article argues that credit-rating methodologies that treat poverty as a proxy for default risk systematically undervalue African sovereign climate risk. For actuaries and financial risk professionals, this signals both a moral imperative and a market opportunity: innovative insurance and risk-transfer structures could unlock significant new capital flows for climate-vulnerable African nations.

Closing data gaps can help Pacific Islands adapt to sea level rise
IN WINDOW 21 SEP 2026 BLOG PHYSICAL RISK
Sea level in much of the western tropical Pacific has risen 10–15 cm since 1993, close to or nearly twice the
global rate, yet only 15.6% of the combined land area of vulnerable atoll nations has LiDAR coverage, only about
three in ten residents of vulnerable coastal areas have ground measurements, and fewer than one in ten square
kilometres of nearshore reef and seabed has been surveyed at all. Tuvalu's 2019 survey captured roughly 500
km² of land, reef and lagoon in about 37 flying hours. Elevation and bathymetry data of this quality is the binding
constraint on credible SIDS flood and inundation models, and its absence is a first-order source of model
uncertainty in parametric and sovereign risk-pool pricing.

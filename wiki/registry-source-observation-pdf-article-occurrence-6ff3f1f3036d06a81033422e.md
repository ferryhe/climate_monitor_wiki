Guide for supervisors: Integrating climate and nature-related risks into prudential supervision
IN WINDOW 21 SEP 2026 GUIDANCE SUPERVISION
The first full rewrite of the NGFS supervisory guide since 2020, walking supervisors through physical and
transition risk transmission, mandate relevance, strategy setting, exposure assessment and the deployment of
micro- and macroprudential tools on a progressive and proportionate basis, evidenced by a member survey
covering 70% of NGFS jurisdictions. For actuaries this is the clearest signal yet that protection-gap and nat-cat
concentration metrics are migrating out of insurance supervision and into bank prudential capital-setting, and
that nature risk is being put on the same supervisory footing as climate.
Caveat. Secondary reporting (Green Central Banking, 25 September 2026) states the guide contemplates capital buffers
for banks with concentrated exposure to climate-vulnerable regions or sectors, extra capital against large mortgage
books in flood-prone areas, LTV adjustments for green assets, and explicit supervisory monitoring of the insurance
protection gap for financial-stability purposes, and reports that about 40% of surveyed members currently consider
nature-related risks. Those four specifics are secondary-sourced: the 2.14 MB NGFS PDF was not opened, so they
should be confirmed against the primary text before being relied upon.

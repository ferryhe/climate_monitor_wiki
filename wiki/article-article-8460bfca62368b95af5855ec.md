## Verified article content

Insurers trade in risk; insurance is, at its heart, a financial risk transfer mechanism. An insurer agrees to bear the financial risk of a specific event occurring. These events – such as a flood – are referred to in insurance as ‘perils﻿’. In return for taking on this risk, in the practice known as ‘underwriting’, the insurer receives a monetary payment or ‘premium’ to cover the cost of potential losses resulting from the insured perils. LSE explains that changing climate hazards make underwriting losses more severe and less predictable, prompting changes in premiums, terms and coverage availability. Climate scenarios, catastrophe models and attribution science can improve assessment, but longer-term insights do not automatically enter short-term contracts. The article also stresses loss-prevention measures and the consequences of insurance retreat.

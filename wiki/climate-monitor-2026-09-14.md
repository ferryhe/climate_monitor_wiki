## Statement: UNEP Report Warns World is Soon to Exceed 1.5°C ‒ but Return is Possible
WASHINGTON (September 2, 2026) — UNEP today published Limiting Overshoot, its first report on the likelihood and consequences of exceeding 1.5°C of global warming. It finds that global temperature rise is set to cross 1.5°C, likely within the next few years, pushing climate risks to increasingly dangerous levels. The report also finds that temperatures can still be brought back down in line with the long-term goals of the Paris Agreement.

## UNDP and Jupiter Intelligence Partner to Boost Climate Resilience in Developing Countries
UNDP and Jupiter Intelligence are partnering to provide developing countries with climate-risk data and analytics covering hazards including extreme heat, precipitation, flooding, and drought. The initiative aims to inform adaptation, public financial management, catastrophe-risk assessment, and the design of insurance risk-finance programs, helping address substantial uninsured climate losses.

## WMO: Invest in Resilience as Climate Risks Intensify
The WMO launched the WMO Commons to mobilize at least US$100 million over five years for weather, climate and water monitoring, forecasting and services that strengthen resilience to intensifying climate risks. The evidence cites US$318 billion in 2024 weather- and climate-related catastrophe losses, only 43% insured, and states that improved forecasts help insurers price risk more accurately.

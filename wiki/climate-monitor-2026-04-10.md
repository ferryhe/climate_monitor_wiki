## [CAS RFP] Developing Actuarial Frameworks to Quantify and Manage Climate Risk
The Casualty Actuarial Society (CAS), through its Climate and Sustainability Working Group, is soliciting research proposals and awarding up to two projects that support the development of innovative actuarial approaches to understanding, quantifying, and managing the climate risks facing insurers. While awards in the $40,000-$50,000 range are typical, proposals may request up to $75,000 with clear justification (e.g., data acquisition, model licensing, and/or multi-peril or multi-geography scope). Proposals should be submitted by May 22, 2026.

## [MSCI Institute] Climate Risks Shift from Insurance Challenge to Broader Financial Stability Concern
LONDON / NEW YORK, April 8, 2026 — Physical climate risks are emerging as a significant concern for the global insurance sector, with potential implications for broader financial stability, according to a new report by the MSCI Institute. Rising Losses and Changing Risk Models The report notes that insured losses from natural catastrophes totaled about $107 billion in 2025, marking another year of elevated claims. A substantial share of these losses has been linked to so-called “secondary perils,” including floods, wildfires, and severe storms, rather than singular large-scale events.
